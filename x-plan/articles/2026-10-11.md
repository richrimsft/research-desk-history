## Body

Elon told the X Takeover crowd at Giga Texas yesterday that he still has a lot to learn about chip making. Respect. Most CEOs would never say that out loud.

His plan is that the chip tools all come from the same vendors, so Tesla and SpaceX will customize them and go faster. I think that's the easy half. The hard half is yield.

### What Elon actually said

At the X Takeover event at Giga Texas on Oct 10, hosted by @teslaownersSV, Elon said: "I still have a lot to learn about chip making. Gotta start somewhere. I didn't know how to make cars or rockets. The (chip) equipment's not very customized, it's all coming from the same vendors." He said they'll start with conventional equipment, then build custom tools for much higher wafer throughput. Credit to @SawyerMerritt for the transcript. (https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2109045691842482447)

He also said: "I think we'll learn how to make chips better than anyone." (https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2109046374088216876)

The goal, from the clip @pequityresearch posted: about 10 chip designs a year, when one significant design typically takes 2 years from start to volume. (https://x.com/pequityresearch/status/2109116746753880192) This morning he quoted that and asked world-class chip design and production engineers to join SpaceX or Tesla. (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2109271552378220789)

And he's not handing it to TSMC. On Oct 7: "No, we will build and run the fab. Let there be ZERO doubt about that. Maybe TSMC subleases part of the Terafab if they want, but nothing more than that." (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2107721071571128709) Reuters ran it the same day. (https://www.channelnewsasia.com/business/musk-says-tsmc-wont-run-terafab-ai-chip-complex-6438461)

The scale is wild. 100 million square feet in Grimes County near College Station, $16.8 billion for phase one, 3,000+ jobs, per the Houston Chronicle. (https://www.houstonchronicle.com/news/houston-texas/space/article/terafab-what-to-know-22378844.php) State filings put all four phases at up to $119 billion through 2036, with commercial operations expected in 2028, per ENR. (https://www.enr.com/articles/63809-spacex-records-fight-exposes-119b-terafab-buildout-plans)

[IMAGE: sec1.jpg | Tesla Gigafactory Texas Building 1 near Austin, June 17, 2022. The Oct 10 X Takeover event where Elon talked Terafab was held here. Photo: Larry D. Moore, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### He's right about the machines

Credit where it's due. The tools really do come from a short list. ASML has had a monopoly on EUV machines since the late 2010s, and JPMorgan estimates it held 94% of the whole lithography market in 2025. Its EUV machines cost about $200 million each, and every major chipmaker buys them, TSMC included. Reuters via The Business Times. (https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/startups-tech/technology/asml-extends-chipmaking-dominance-customers-embrace-high-na)

So yeah, Terafab can buy the same machines TSMC buys. Small catch: same Reuters story says they're all but sold out through 2027.

He's also earned some benefit of the doubt. He said "I didn't know how to make cars or rockets" and Tesla and SpaceX both exist.

And there's a smart move people skip over. On Tesla's Q1 call he said Tesla would build a roughly $3 billion research fab at Giga Texas running a few thousand wafer starts a month, "to make sure that a production process is sound." Learn small before going giant. DCD has the quotes. (https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/elon-musk-says-tesla-will-use-intel-14a-technology-at-its-20bn-terafab-project-in-austin/)

[IMAGE: sec2.jpg | ASML air freight containers used to ship its chipmaking machines, parked in Tainan, Taiwan, May 27, 2025. Photo: 4300streetcar, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### Same machines, different results

Yield is the share of chips on a wafer that actually work. You can't order it from ASML.

Look at TSMC in Arizona. Same company, same tools, same process. Construction started in 2021, and volume production still slipped to early 2025, blamed on a lack of construction workers and engineers. When the yield finally came in, it was about 4% better than Taiwan. How? TSMC moved hundreds of Taiwanese engineers and their families to Phoenix. Credit to Electronics Weekly. (https://www.electronicsweekly.com/news/business/manufacturing/tsmcs-arizona-yield-4-better-than-in-taiwan-2024-10/)

Same tools plus the same people got the same yield. The people were the secret.

TSMC CEO C.C. Wei on the April earnings call: "It takes two to three years to build a new fab. No shortcuts. And it takes another one to two years to ramp it up." (https://www.electronicsweekly.com/blogs/mannerisms/manuf/terafab-2026-04/) That's from a company that has been doing this since 1987. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSMC)

10 designs a year sounds amazing. Every one of them still has to yield.

[IMAGE: sec3.jpg | TSMC fab buildings in Hsinchu Science Park, Taiwan, where TSMC built its process know-how. Photo: 曾 成訓, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### Intel is the warning

Intel has made chips for decades, buys from ASML like everyone else, and was the early adopter of ASML's newest High NA machines. Its foundry business still lost $10.3 billion in 2025 and $13.3 billion in 2024, per Intel's own annual report. (https://www.intc.com/filings-reports/all-sec-filings/xbrl_doc_only/4151)

Now the twist. Terafab is betting on Intel's 14A process. On the Q1 call Elon called it "state-of-the-art and in fact not yet totally complete," and said it should be mature "by the time TeraFab scales up." (https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/elon-musk-says-tesla-will-use-intel-14a-technology-at-its-20bn-terafab-project-in-austin/)

So the plan is a brand new team, on a process that isn't finished, at a scale nobody has tried. I'd love to be wrong. He's made me wrong before. But @StragglerLiu nailed it this morning: "Capital can buy a fab. It cannot buy yield." (https://x.com/StragglerLiu/status/2109196640783675408)

So which is it? Is the car and rocket playbook enough to crack chip yield in a few years, or is this the first thing Elon can't speed run?
