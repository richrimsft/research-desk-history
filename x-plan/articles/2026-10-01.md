## Article body

The UAP community wanted a smoking gun. What it got in September was a file dump.

On Sept. 18, 2026, the Pentagon put out its sixth batch of Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena records under PURSUE — the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. Seventy-one files. PDFs, videos, one long audio tape. Most of them redacted. Dates spanning 1952 to 2025.

That’s useful. It is not a verdict.

### What actually dropped

CBS News counted the sixth release at 71 files: 67 from Defense, four from Colorado law enforcement. Fifty-five PDFs, 15 videos, one audio file. Sixty-four carry redactions. ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ufo-files-uap-pentagon-6th-release/))

Coverage puts the running public total around 447 files across six PURSUE tranches, with the first drop on May 8, 2026. The official portal is [war.gov/ufo](https://www.war.gov/ufo/).

If you only read headlines, you’d think “aliens.” If you open the folder, you get paperwork, sensor clips, and a lot of “still unexplained.”

### The AAWSAP papers are not crash retrievals

The flashiest documents in this batch are 37 Defense Intelligence Reference Documents from AAWSAP — the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program the DIA ran from 2008 to 2012. Topics include warp drives, wormholes, antigravity, negative mass propulsion, and cloaking.

Important receipt: those DIRDs were already public for years through FOIA. Friday’s versions mainly restore details that used to be stripped, like which offices produced them. The Pentagon’s own notes say they are “reference and synthesis products rather than original research” and “should not be read as implying current validation of the concepts discussed.” ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ufo-files-uap-pentagon-6th-release/))

One 2010 paper on injuries to “antennae engineers” after an anomalous aerospace-linked event is clinical and ugly. It supports a hypothesis that some advanced systems may already be deployed and poorly understood. It does not name a craft, a country, or a species.

### The videos: objects on sensors, no caption saying “ET”

Friday’s videos look like the ones you’ve already seen: grainy military cams, objects cutting across the frame. One 2022 Middle East mission report describes a UAP moving between about 80 and 180 mph. Another shows roughly six small spherical objects that “seemed to be agile” and changed direction often. Both remain unexplained in the release notes. ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ufo-files-uap-pentagon-6th-release/))

Four cellphone clips came from Colorado law enforcement in October 2023 — silent lights, red/blue/green flashes, partially blurred. Local LE to AARO is a reporting path. It is not a lab result.

Historical cut: an hour of Edward Ruppelt audio from 1952. He said roughly 20% of about 800 Air Force UFO reports still had “absolutely no conclusions.” Project Blue Book material is in the dump too. Mystery rate ≠ origin.

### Congress is still asking for the hard drives

Document dumps and congressional demands are not the same thing.

On April 1, 2026, House Oversight’s Task Force on the Declassification of Federal Secrets (Chair Anna Paulina Luna) sent a letter to the Department of War requesting specific UAP video files whistleblowers said AARO still holds — formations, instant-acceleration clips, USO footage, and more — with a delivery deadline. ([Oversight release](https://oversight.house.gov/release/luna-continues-transparency-investigation-into-uaps/), [letter PDF](https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/UAP-Request-Letter-FINAL.pdf))

AARO’s public case work still resolves many famous clips as balloons, look-angle artifacts, or ordinary objects when the data is good enough. ([AARO case resolutions](https://www.aaro.mil/UAP-Cases/UAP-Case-Resolution-Reports/)) That office’s long-standing line: no confirmed extraterrestrial technology in the cases it has closed.

You can believe the sky has unexplained things and still demand chain of custody. Those are not opposite positions.

### Closing

PURSUE is doing something governments usually avoid: putting the folder online in public. That matters. Redactions, recycled DIRDs, and “unexplained” sensor tracks also matter.

Claims deserve checking. Uncatalogued still isn’t aliens. Show provenance, keep the receipts, and don’t confuse a declassification portal with a disclosure press conference.

Question: if the next tranche still has no chain-of-custody hardware, do you change the ask — or just raise the volume?
