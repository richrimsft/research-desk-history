## Body

Elon says Digital Optimus can get about halfway through Diablo just by watching the screen. Half my feed is acting like an AI just beat a video game for the first time.

It didn't. AI has been beating games from raw pixels since 2015. Playing isn't the question anymore. Working is.

### What Elon actually said

At 3:34am ET, replying to @teslayoda, Elon posted: "Digital Optimus can play about halfway through the Diablo campaign so far just by looking at the screen like a human. Skill at Counter-Strike and other fast games is good too. League is also in training. Goal is to generalize across all games." (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2108823477071303109)

At 10:25am ET he quoted it: "Join @TeslaSI if you're interested in solving generalized real-world super intelligence!" (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2108926853230493843)

All of this started with @teslayoda spotting a new Tesla job: Machine Learning Engineer, Realtime Games, Digital Optimus, in Palo Alto. Build agents that play AAA games, browse the web and use pro apps at superhuman levels. Good catch. (https://x.com/teslayoda/status/2108779986173993233)

Quick refresher. Digital Optimus, also called Macrohard, is the joint xAI and Tesla project Elon announced on March 11. Grok directs, and Digital Optimus handles the past 5 seconds of screen video and keyboard and mouse actions. (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2031751255060885911) CNBC covered it the same day. (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/11/musk-unveils-joint-tesla-xai-project-macrohard.html)

No video. No benchmark. Just a post. So for now it's Elon's claim, and I'm treating it like one.

### AI beat games years ago

2015. DeepMind's DQN got nothing but the pixels and the score and reached a level comparable to a professional human games tester across 49 Atari games. Same algorithm for all of them. (https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236)

2019. DeepMind's AlphaStar hit Grandmaster in StarCraft II with all three races, above 99.8% of officially ranked human players. (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z) It played through a camera view with capped actions, rules a pro player signed off on. (https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/alphastar-grandmaster-level-in-starcraft-ii-using-multi-agent-reinforcement-learning/)

Also 2019. OpenAI Five beat OG, the reigning Dota 2 world champions. What people forget: it never looked at the pixels. It read the game's data directly, about 16,000 values every step. (https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)

On paper, halfway through a single-player campaign is a lower bar than the StarCraft ladder. Unless it's one model doing all of it. That part we don't know yet.

### "Like a human" isn't new either

Looking at the screen and pressing keys like a person? DeepMind's SIMA did that in March 2024. Screen images and text instructions in, keyboard and mouse out, no game source code, no special APIs, nine games from eight studios. (https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/sima-generalist-ai-agent-for-3d-virtual-environments/)

SIMA 2 in November 2025 put Gemini inside, so it reasons about goals and still plays by looking at the screen with a virtual keyboard and mouse. (https://deepmind.google/blog/sima-2-an-agent-that-plays-reasons-and-learns-with-you-in-virtual-3d-worlds/)

For actual computer work, Anthropic gave Claude computer use in October 2024 and scored 14.9% on the OSWorld desktop benchmark, screenshot only. (https://www.anthropic.com/news/3-5-models-and-computer-use) OpenAI's Computer-Using Agent behind Operator hit 38.1% in January 2025. Humans score 72.4%. (https://openai.com/index/computer-using-agent/)

Credit to all three. They published their numbers.

[IMAGE: sec3.jpg | The crowd at the BlizzCon 2011 StarCraft II Invitational. AlphaStar reached Grandmaster in StarCraft II in 2019. Photo: My Blue Van, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### What would actually be new

Speed. Those desktop agents run a screenshot, think, click loop. OpenAI lays it out in its own write-up. (https://openai.com/index/computer-using-agent/) Counter-Strike doesn't wait for you to think. If Digital Optimus really plays fast shooters in real time on what Elon calls a $650 Tesla AI4 chip, that's different. He called it "the only real-time smart AI system." (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2031751255060885911)

One model for every game. That's Elon's stated goal. Even DeepMind says SIMA needs more research to reach human level in games it knows and games it's never seen. (https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/sima-generalist-ai-agent-for-3d-virtual-environments/)

And proof. Two years ago today, Optimus robots worked the crowd at Tesla's We, Robot event. Bloomberg later reported employees remotely oversaw many of those interactions, and Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas wrote they "relied on tele-ops (human intervention)." Credit to Rebecca Bellan at TechCrunch for pulling it together. (https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/14/tesla-optimus-bots-were-controlled-by-humans-during-the-we-robot-event/) So yeah, I want footage.

@niccruzpatane says Digital Optimus will eventually handle general computer work. I agree that's the prize. (https://x.com/niccruzpatane/status/2108920810584875187) But the test isn't Diablo. It's a messy spreadsheet, a broken login page and a surprise update popup, all at once.

So which is it? Is a game-playing AI the road to doing your job, or just a cool demo until Tesla shows it on a real desktop?
