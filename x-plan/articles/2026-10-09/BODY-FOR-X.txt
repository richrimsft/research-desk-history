SpaceX and Tesla are worth more together right now than the US government borrowed in all of fiscal 2026. Almost twice as much.

My math, not Elon's. Not investment advice, just math.

### The math

As of 10:36am ET today, Oct 9, SpaceX (SPCX) is at $163.82. Market cap about $2.23 trillion. (https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/spcx)

Tesla (TSLA) is at $386.96. About $1.53 trillion. (https://www.nasdaq.com/market-activity/stocks/tsla)

Add them up and you get about $3.75 trillion.

The FY2026 federal deficit came in at $2.0 trillion. That's CBO's number, reported by the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget. (https://www.crfb.org/press-releases/cbo-estimates-20-trillion-deficit-fiscal-year-2026)

$3.75T vs $2.0T. That's 1.88x. Two companies, almost two full years of deficit.

And no, this isn't the national debt. Different number. I'm only talking about one year of borrowing.

### Starlink is the money printer

Starlink subscribers doubled to 12 million in a year. SpaceX's connectivity segment, mostly Starlink, made about $5.3 billion in operating income over the last four quarters. All of Tesla made about $4.4 billion over the same stretch. Credit to Daniel Sparks at Motley Fool for lining that up. (https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/09/spacex-is-worth-about-usd800-billion-more-than-tesla-on-less-than-a-quarter-of-its-sales/)

A satellite internet business out-earning the whole car company. Wild.

And it's coming for your phone next. SpaceX signed a deal to buy Grain Management's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum for Starlink Mobile. It still needs FCC approval, so it's not done yet. (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/grain-management-announces-definitive-agreement-to-sell-nationwide-800-mhz-spectrum-portfolio-to-spacex-302902974.html)

### The comeback nobody priced in

CNBC on Oct 5: SpaceX shares climbed about 58% from the early August bottom, after a week with a crewed NASA mission and Google's AI chips sent to orbit. (https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/05/spacex-stock-climbs-highest-since-june-returning-musk-to-trillionaire.html)

Morgan Stanley's Adam Jonas kept his $300 target and called SpaceX "cheap and getting cheaper." (https://finviz.com/news/398657/morgan-stanley-reiterates-300-spacex-target-citing-growth-adjusted-valuation)

Fair is fair though. The stock fell 4.2% yesterday to $160.57, per MarketBeat. It doesn't go up in a straight line. (https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/price-spacex-nasdaq-spcx-stock-falls-42-whats-next-2026-10-08/)

### Tesla beat the Street

Tesla delivered 486,532 cars in Q3. Per Fred Lambert at Electrek, that's about 25,000 over the 461,974 consensus, roughly 5% above. It beat every estimate on the list. Still down 2.1% from last year's record, to be fair. (https://electrek.co/2026/10/02/tesla-q3-2026-deliveries-486532/)

People keep calling Tesla a dead car company. Dead car companies don't beat every estimate.

### Elon's "super crazy" path, and his own fine print

Yesterday Elon posted: "This will sound super crazy, but I see a path to SpaceX being worth orders of magnitude more than the current Earth economy." (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2108315261044445330) Benzinga picked it up fast. (https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/10/62266293/elon-musk-says-spcx-could-be-worth-orders-of-magnitude-more-than-the-entire-earth-economy-super-crazy)

Then at 10:14am ET today he added the fine print himself: "'Seeing a path' is not a statement of extreme confidence that we will achieve that outcome. Just means that I believe there is such a path and the probability of achieving it is not zero." (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2108561824400691429)

Respect for saying that out loud. Not zero isn't a promise.

My take: you don't need the crazy number. Two of his companies already outweigh a full year of federal borrowing. That's the story.

So is $3.75 trillion for two Musk companies earned, or is it hype?
