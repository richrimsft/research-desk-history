A guy from Belgium looked at the ice under the South Pole and decided it was a telescope. Today he won the Nobel Prize for it. The whole thing, by himself.

Coolest physics Nobel in years. Not close.

### One name on the prize

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics went to Francis Halzen of UW-Madison "for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin" (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/press-release/)

First time since 1992 the physics prize went to just one person. (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03092-1)

He's 82. They reached him by phone in Italy. "It was a great surprise, and I obviously didn't expect it." Then he said he's working on a proposal and hopes the prize helps get it approved. (https://www.the-journal.com/articles/associated-press/nobel-prize-in-physics-to-be-awarded-in-stockholm/)

Legend.

### He turned a glacier into a telescope

Neutrinos are ghost particles. They fly through the whole Earth and through you and you never notice. (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/press-release/)

So you need a ridiculous amount of stuff for them to hit. He pitched using the South Pole ice in 1988. IceCube ended up as a cubic kilometer of Antarctic ice with 5,160 light sensors on 86 cables, finished in 2011. The holes get melted with hot water. (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/popular-information/)

[IMAGE: sec2.jpg - IceCube hot water drill camp at the South Pole, December 2009. Photo: Amble, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### Nobody thought it would work

His words today: "no respectable conservative physicist would have joined me, but many talented people did and that's why I'm here." (https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03092-1)

Last year he said it started as a "cute" idea and "Many people thought we wouldn't, but we did." (https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/awards/2025/12/prof-francis-halzen-honored-with-the-2026-aps-medal/)

The first sensors were a letdown because the upper ice was full of bubbles. He spent Christmas Eve 1993 at the dinner table with his laptop, waiting to hear if the first cable made it in. (https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2026/popular-information/)

[IMAGE: sec3.jpg - An IceCube digital optical module waiting to go down hole 85, December 2009. Photo: Amble, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### Then it worked

2013: first high-energy neutrinos from outside the solar system. (https://news.wisc.edu/university-of-wisconsin-madison-professor-francis-halzen-named-2026-nobel-laureate-in-physics/)

2018: they traced one to a blazar called TXS 0506+056. (https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/press-releases/2018/07/icecube-neutrinos-point-to-long-sought-cosmic-ray-accelerator/)

2023: the first picture of our own Milky Way made with neutrinos instead of light. (https://icecube.wisc.edu/news/press-releases/2023/06/our-galaxy-seen-through-a-new-lens-neutrinos-detected-by-icecube/)

Idea in 1988. Nobel in 2026. That's a 38 year bet and he won it.

I'll take this over another prize nobody can explain at dinner.

So be honest. Would you have signed up in 1992, or been one of the guys calling it cute?
