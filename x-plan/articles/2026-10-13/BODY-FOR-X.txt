A year ago the $7,500 federal EV credit died and a lot of people figured that was Tesla's problem. Competitors were finally going to catch up.

Kelley Blue Book just dropped the Q3 numbers. Tesla sold an estimated 144,094 EVs in the US and took 54% of the whole market. More than every other EV brand combined. (https://www.coxautoinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Q3-2026-Kelley-Blue-Book-EV-Sales-Report-updated-as-of-10-12-26.pdf)

### The credit ended. Everyone else fell harder.

Q3 2025 was the rush quarter. People were buying before the credit expired Sept 30. Tesla had about 41% of US EV sales then (179,525 of 434,279 in KBB's own table). This Q3, no credit at all: 54%. Same PDF.

Tesla was down 19.7% year over year. That's not nothing. But the whole EV market was down 38.6%. Ford EVs down 80.2%. Chevy down 74.6%. VW down 90.5%. (https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/us-ev-sales-q3-2026-tesla-market-share/)

F-150 Lightning sold 289 in Q3. The Cybertruck everyone loves to dunk on sold 4,323. Both straight from the KBB model table.

### The EV market is mostly one car

Model Y did 99,037 in Q3. That's 37.2% of every EV sold in America. One car.

The best-selling EV not made by Tesla was the Hyundai Ioniq 5 at 10,382. Model Y did about 9.5x that. Sawyer Merritt called it 9x this morning and he's right. (https://x.com/SawyerMerritt/status/2110021304162078893)

This one got me. Model Y alone outsold GM (Chevy, Cadillac and GMC), Ford, Hyundai, Kia, Toyota, Lexus, Honda and Rivian combined. 99,037 vs 98,769 by my math off KBB's brand table. They're estimates, so call it a tie if you want. Still insane.

Year to date it's 262,491 Model Ys, down only 1% from last year when the credit still existed. (https://electrek.co/2026/10/12/top-10-best-selling-evs-us-through-q3/)

[IMAGE: sec2.jpg | 2025 Tesla Model Y in the Washington DC metro area. Photo: OWS Photography, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### Fair point for the bears

Tesla is basically a two car company now. Cox says Model Y was 69% of Tesla's US sales in Q3 and Model 3 was 28%. (https://teslanewswire.com/articles/tesla-model-y-model-3-top-us-ev-sales-q3-2026-kbb)

Model S sold 215 for the whole quarter. Model X sold 173. Model 3 was down 25.1% from a year ago. If Model Y ever stumbles there's no backup car. (KBB PDF)

And these are KBB estimates, not Tesla's own US numbers. Tesla doesn't break those out.

[IMAGE: sec3.jpg | Tesla Model 3 (Highland) in the Washington DC metro area. Photo: OWS Photography, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons]

### Who actually showed up

Not everyone flopped. Rivian was up 45.7% to 19,239, with the new R2 (3,456) and way more delivery vans (8,469 vs 2,639 a year ago). Toyota EVs up 328%. Lexus up 442%. (KBB PDF)

The market overall is climbing back too. 266,630 EVs in Q3, up 7.7% from Q2 and the best quarter since the credit rush. EVs were 6.5% of all new US vehicle sales, up from 5.8% in Q2. Cox's Stephanie Valdez Streaty says the reset phase is "largely behind us." (https://electrek.co/2026/10/12/top-10-best-selling-evs-us-through-q3/) (https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/us-ev-sales-q3-2026-tesla-market-share/)

My take. The credit never made Tesla. It kept a lot of other EVs looking cheaper than they were. The brands that grew this year did it with new cars people actually wanted, not a tax break.

So was the EV credit ever helping Tesla, or was it just keeping everyone else in the race?
